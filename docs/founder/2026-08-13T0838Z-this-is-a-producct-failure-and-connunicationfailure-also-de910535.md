What "AI-Generated" Breaks
1.  Every "Sourced Claim" Is Now Suspect
Your copy says: "Every claim links to its source."
AI citations are notoriously unreliable. LLMs hallucinate URLs, misattribute quotes, and misread data. If a customer clicks a source link and it is dead, wrong, or does not say what the document claims it says, your credibility is permanently destroyed.
You need a human verification layer. Not for the writing — for the links. Every citation in every pack needs to be spot-checked by a human before it ships. If you cannot afford that, change the promise to "Sources provided for major claims; verified where possible" and be explicit about the confidence level.
2.  The Financial Model Is Dangerous
An AI-generated financial model with a disclaimer that "anything we could not verify is marked missing" sounds responsible, but an AI does not know what it does not know. It will confidently fill gaps with plausible-sounding numbers.
A first-time founder who takes your AI-generated pricing and margin assumptions to a supplier, gets laughed out of the room, and loses £500 on a bad order — they will blame you. And they will be right to.
Fix: Add a bright red banner at the top of every financial document: "This model was generated by AI and has not been verified by an accountant or industry expert. Treat all numbers as hypotheses, not facts. Verify every assumption before spending money."
3.  The Build Spec Could Get Someone Hacked
You are recommending tech stacks. If the AI suggests a deprecated package with a known vulnerability, or a no-code tool that has changed its pricing model, or a compliance step that is legally wrong for a specific jurisdiction, you are exposed.
Fix: Build specs need a technical review gate. Even if it is just you running the output through a checklist. Or restrict build specs to "widely adopted, well-documented tools only" and avoid niche recommendations.
4.  The "Kill Log" Could Be Nonsense
"The value would not last" and "Incumbents already own the space" are verdicts. If these are AI-generated summaries of scraped data, they can be superficially correct but deeply wrong. An AI might kill an idea because Crunchbase shows two funded competitors, missing that both are failing and the market is ripe for a bootstrapped alternative.
Fix: Publish your scoring rubric. Not the AI prompt — the rubric. What signals get weighted? How is "market saturation" defined? If your kill logic is transparent, people can disagree with it intelligently instead of discovering it is a black box.
