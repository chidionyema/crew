In a 100/100 Backstage setup, the transition from looking at the entire "Estate" (the macro view) to a specific service's "Details" (the micro view) is completely seamless. You never have to cross-reference a spreadsheet, log into the AWS console, and then open GitHub to piece the story together.Here is exactly how that macro-to-micro workflow looks and functions.1. The Macro: The Estate Inventory (Software Catalog)When you first open the catalog to view the estate, you aren't just looking at a static list of microservices. You are looking at a living, auto-discovered inventory.Faceted Filtering: You see a master table of everything, but it is heavily filterable. You can instantly filter the 1,000+ components down to: "Show me all Tier-1 backend services, owned by the Checkout Team, that are currently in production, built with Go."Unified Abstraction: The inventory doesn't just show microservices. It tracks APIs, Machine Learning Models, Data Pipelines, and Infrastructure (like Kafka clusters or S3 buckets) as first-class citizens in the exact same view.System Groupings: Services aren't isolated; they are grouped into "Systems" and "Domains." A Founder or Architecture Lead can look at the "Payments Domain" and see the 15 microservices and 4 databases that make it up, along with an aggregated health score for that entire domain.2. The Transition: The Topology GraphThe most elite way to go from the "Estate" to the "Details" is visually, through a dependency graph. Because Backstage relies on YAML metadata that declares dependsOn and providesApi, it automatically draws a live map of your architecture.Key insight: You don't just search for a service by name. If the checkout-UI is throwing errors, an engineer can look at the graph, see that checkout-UI depends on payment-gateway, see that payment-gateway is flagged red, and click it to drill down.3. The Micro: The Details (The Entity Dashboard)Once you click into a specific piece of the inventory (e.g., the payment-gateway service), you enter the Entity View. This is where the magic of the 100/100 setup shines. Backstage aggregates the details from 10 different SaaS tools into one single pane of glass for this specific service.Here is what the detail tabs look like:The "Overview" TabThe Vitals: Who owns it, its PagerDuty rotation (with a "Page the On-Call" button), and links to the GitHub repo and Jira board.Cost Insights: A widget showing exactly how much this specific service cost to run in AWS/GCP over the last 30 days, and whether it is trending up or down.The "CI/CD" TabYou see the live GitHub Actions workflow or GitLab CI pipeline. You can see the exact commit SHA that is currently building.If a build failed, you can read the build logs directly inside Backstage without switching tabs.The "Kubernetes" TabYou see the live production and staging clusters.It shows the exact pods running. If a pod is in a CrashLoopBackOff, it is highlighted in red. You can view the real-time tail logs of that failing pod, or restart the deployment directly from the UI.The "APIs & Docs" TabTechDocs: The Markdown documentation stored in the repo is rendered beautifully here.API Specs: If the service exposes a REST or GraphQL API, the Swagger UI or Apollo Explorer is embedded right here. A developer from another team can instantly see the endpoints and test a mock payload.The "Security" TabA localized view of vulnerabilities (from Trivy, Snyk, or SonarQube) strictly for this repository. It tells the team exactly which dependencies need to be patched to clear their security debt.
