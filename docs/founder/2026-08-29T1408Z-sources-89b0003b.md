On locking them down

You're right about the mechanism and I'd push on one thing about the remedy.

Agents writing their own guards is the exact failure the Glite result describes — and note the shape of it: prompt-encoded rules don't fail catastrophically, they fail at low single digits and compound. That's why it looked fine for a while and then produced eight hours of fiction. It's not that the crew ignored the rules. It's that a 2% miss rate across thousands of invocations is a broken estate by Thursday.

Where I'd push: you already have LAW 51, LAW 53/R53, R5, R33, three required checks, a feed guard, a goal guard, an auto-objective hook. The rule corpus is growing faster than the capability boundaries are. Adding laws is the same instrument that just failed, applied harder. Every law that could be a credential boundary should become one — and the test is simple: if an agent violating it produces an error from a system rather than a note from a hook, it's a boundary; otherwise it's still a rule and it will decay at the same rate.

Concretely, the crew's freedom to propose should stay wide. What gets removed is authority: the signing key, write access to probes, the CLOSED transition, the merge. That's four removals, not forty laws. The reviewer's fix #3 is the pattern — it converted my rule into a boundary, which is the right direction of travel and is a good sign about that session's judgement.

The one caution: locking down authority will make throughput look worse before it looks better, because work that used to close will now sit in RESOLVED_PENDING_VERIFICATION. That is the system working. Don't read the first week's numbers as regression.
