### 2. The commit history is a signed confession that the code may not be copyrightable

**Hypothesis.** The crew history carries machine co-author trailers (`Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 5`, `Claude Fable 5` — 222 matching lines across 168 commits, measured this session), and across the 5 live repos more than 40% of commits carry one — so the estate has voluntarily created the machine-readable evidence a buyer's counsel needs to argue that a large share of the codebase lacks human authorship and cannot be conveyed as owned copyright.

**Why it matters.** If true, the IP representation in the sale agreement has to be rewritten from "we own it" to "we transfer every right we hold, plus the trade secrets and know-how" before the buyer's lawyer finds it — and finding it yourself is worth far more than them finding it.

**Test.** Data: `git log --format='%H%n%b'` in all 5 repos. Method: count commits with a machine trailer, and cross-reference against the US Copyright Office's January 2025 position that works without meaningful human creative input are not copyrightable and that prompt-writing alone does not confer authorship ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/02/copyright-office-publishes-report), [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/02/copyrightability-of-ai-outputs-us-copyright-office-analyzes-human-authorship-requirement)). Threshold: >40% of commits carrying a machine trailer confirms it. Cost: 1 hour, £0. Then 1 hour of an IP solicitor, ~£300, on whether the trailers are helpful (provenance) or harmful (admission).

**Prior. 0.7.** The trailer convention is mandated estate-wide by `~/AGENTS.md`, so the rate is likely high; what is uncertain is whether counsel treats it as an admission or as ordinary tooling metadata.

**What would change my mind.** An IP lawyer confirming that co-author trailers are read as tool attribution, like a compiler, and never as evidence of absent human authorship.

**Do not act on this by deleting the trailers.** Removing them after the question is raised is spoliation and converts a legal argument into a credibility problem.

**What would change my mind.** A clean SBOM on prospector, the largest tree at 73,906 lines, which would make the rest very likely clean too.

**Hypothesis.** Provider audit logs for the credentials found by gitleaks across the 11 repos (#240) show at least one authenticated call from an IP or user-agent nobody on the estate recognises, dated before 2026-08-25.

**Why it matters.** It is the difference between a hygiene task and a disclosure obligation — a used credential is an incident that must be told to a buyer, and telling them late is worse than telling them at all.

**Test.** Borrowed from insurance claims handling: check whether the loss already occurred before pricing the cover. Method: for each provider with an audit log (Anthropic, OpenAI, GitHub, cloud), pull 90 days and diff source IPs against the estate's known set. Threshold: one unrecognised authenticated call confirms. Cost: 3 hours, £0. Context for why the register is not enough on its own: 64% of credentials confirmed valid in 2022 were still exploitable when retested in January 2026 ([GitGuardian](https://blog.gitguardian.com/the-state-of-secrets-sprawl-2026/), [Snyk](https://snyk.io/articles/state-of-secrets/)) — rotation is not something organisations actually complete.

**Prior. 0.2.** Low base rate for a small estate, but the consequence is asymmetric and the check is three hours.

**What would change my mind.** Finding that the majority of the 660 findings are in repos that were private for their whole history, which collapses the exposure window.

**K2. "Set up source-code escrow to reassure the buyer."**
Killer test: 10 minutes of reading. **Result: dead.** Escrow protects a *licensee* against a vendor's insolvency. In an asset sale the buyer receives the source outright, so escrow answers a question nobody is asking. Included because it is exactly the kind of answer that sounds sophisticated and is structurally irrelevant.

**K3. "The 3,608 dossiers and 166k ledger rows are an asset a buyer pays for."**

They are a baseline to beat, which is what R31/R32 already say.
