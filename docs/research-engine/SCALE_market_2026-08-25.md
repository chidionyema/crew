# Scale: market. Target: the UK pre-commitment venture decision market (prospector's market)

Generated by the research engine from PROMPT_generate.md, 2026-08-25, model claude-fable-5, 12 web searches max. No dossier was read.

**The market:** Prospector serves the UK *pre-commitment venture decision* market — people about to put money or months into one specific business idea (solo side-hustler, 1–20 staff owner, growth operator, venture founder, per the four lanes), where the decision being sold is go/no-go and the checks that frame it (`payer_solvency`, `distribution`, `incumbency`, `legality`) say the real question is not "is this a nice idea" but "is there a buyer with money I can legally reach".

**TARGET:** that market. **SCALE:** market.

---

## 1. The money in this market sits with people who already own an idea, not people shopping for one

**Hypothesis.** By the end of 2026, willingness to pay for "vet the idea I already have" is at least 5x, per visitor, the willingness to pay for "browse ideas someone else vetted", because the browsing population is pre-revenue and the owning population has already spent money it wants to defend.

**Why it matters.** It flips what is sold: the catalogue of vetted opportunities becomes a marketing asset and the paid product becomes a single-subject verdict commissioned by the idea's owner — which also flips the pricing ladder off info-product rungs onto professional-service rungs.

**Test.** Two landing pages, same traffic source, same price rung: "Ideas we could not kill" vs "Send us your idea. We try to kill it." Measure paid conversion, not clicks. Pass if the commissioned page converts at ≥3x over 300 visitors per arm; fail if <1.5x. Cost: about £250 of paid traffic and 6 hours. The precedent to read while it runs is the credit-rating industry's move from subscriber-pays to issuer-pays in the 1970s, and the finding that the flip does not remove bias but changes its direction ([Bonsall et al., *Journal of Accounting and Economics*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165410123000381); [Capital Markets Law Journal](https://academic.oup.com/cmlj/article/17/3/334/6609964)).

**Prior. 0.6** — every adjacent market that survived (valuations, surveys, credit ratings, franchise disclosure) is paid for by the party with something already at stake.

**What would change my mind.** A commissioned-verdict page that converts below the browse page at equal traffic, which would say the buyer wants permission to start rather than a test of a commitment already made.

## 2. In the biggest lane, the acute pain has moved from choosing the idea to surviving the paperwork

**Hypothesis.** For the side-hustle lane, more than 30% of the questions being asked in public UK forums in 2026 are tax, registration and platform-reporting questions, not idea-selection questions — because HMRC received income reports on **3,988,892** online sellers totalling **almost £55bn for calendar 2025, more than double the £25.5bn for 2024** ([BDO](https://www.bdo.co.uk/en-gb/news/2026/hmrc-plans-fresh-tax-crackdown-after-receiving-income-data-on-4m-online-sellers), [IFA Magazine](https://ifamagazine.com/hmrc-plans-fresh-tax-crackdown-after-receiving-income-data-on-4m-online-sellers/)), and the reporting duty has run since 1 January 2024 ([ICAEW](https://www.icaew.com/insights/tax-news/2024/aug-2024/new-hmrc-guidance-on-digital-platforms)).

**Why it matters.** If true, the `legality` check is not a gate on the product — it *is* the product for the lane with the largest population, and the sellable unit becomes an idea plus its compliance route rather than an idea plus a score.

**Test.** Sample 300 posts from three named public sources (r/UKPersonalFinance, r/smallbusinessuk, the UK Business Forums "Startup" board) over a fixed 30-day window, code each post as idea-selection / demand / compliance / operations, two coders on a 50-post overlap for agreement. Pass at ≥30% compliance; fail below 15%. Cost: 5 hours, £0.

**Prior. 0.55** — the doubling of reported income and the Chartered Institute of Taxation's own warning of a side-hustle "time-bomb" ([CIOT](https://www.tax.org.uk/hmrc-must-act-now-to-defuse-side-hustle-time-bomb)) both point at a population that has already started and is now frightened, not one still choosing.

**What would change my mind.** Compliance posts under 15% of the sample, which would mean the fear is real but is being taken to accountants rather than expressed as demand.

## 3. The free top of the funnel for this market closes during 2026

**Hypothesis.** By December 2026, more than 60% of UK search queries of the form "is X a good business to start" return an AI answer that resolves the question on the results page, so organic search referrals to any opportunity-research site fall by at least 40% year on year.

**Why it matters.** It decides whether distribution is earned (content and search) or bought and owned (a named list, a partner's customer base, a channel) — and the answer changes the entire cost structure, not just the marketing plan.

**Test.** Take 100 real queries in this shape, run them through a rank tracker or by hand from a UK IP in one sitting, record whether an AI answer appears and whether any independent site is cited above the fold. Pass at ≥60% coverage. Cost: 3 hours, or about £30 on a tracker's trial. The baseline to compare against is Pew's tracked-behaviour study: users clicked a result **8% of the time when an AI summary was present versus 15% without**, only **1%** of AI summaries produced a click on a cited source, and **18%** of searches triggered one in March 2025 ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-hurting-clicks-study-459434), [Marketing Charts](https://www.marketingcharts.com/digital-236587)).

**Prior. 0.7** — coverage was already 18% of all queries a year ago and this query shape is exactly the informational kind that gets summarised.

**What would change my mind.** Coverage under 30% on the 100-query sample, which would mean commercial-intent business queries are being deliberately left alone.

## 4. The solvent payer in this market is the franchisor, not the founder

**Hypothesis.** UK franchisors already pay £50–£400 for a qualified franchisee lead, and a vetted-candidate feed would clear £100 per lead — in a sector reported at **48,600 franchise businesses, £17.2bn and 710,000 jobs**, up from 44,000 outlets and £15.1bn in the prior survey ([British Franchise Association industry research](https://www.thebfa.org/industry-research/), [NatWest/bfa coverage](https://thecreationstation.co.uk/franchising-continues-to-grow-at-record-levels-according-to-the-latest-natwest-research/)).

**Why it matters.** It converts a consumer product with a one-off £30–£199 ticket into a business-to-business feed with recurring revenue, and it means the engine's kills become the qualification step someone else is already paying for.

**Test.** Fifteen calls or emails to bfa-member franchisors and two franchise lead portals asking one question: what do you currently pay per qualified enquiry, and what counts as qualified. Pass if ≥8 give a number and the median is ≥£100. Cost: 6 hours, £0. Note the trade body's own claim that the sector's commercial failure rate fell from 0.9% to 0.5% ([LondonWorld](https://www.londonworld.com/business/sw-london-franchisee-backs-survey-franchises-boom-with-995-success-rate-4837361)) is self-reported by the industry and should be treated as marketing, not as a survival statistic — the comparable independent figure is ONS's **38.4% five-year survival** (below).

**Prior. 0.45** — the sector is large and demonstrably buys leads, but franchisors may treat an outside vetting layer as competition for their own discovery call.

**What would change my mind.** Franchisors naming a price but insisting the lead must come from their own funnel to be usable, which would make this a channel we cannot enter.

## 5. Demand here is deadline-shaped, and the deadlines are known in advance

**Hypothesis.** UK search interest in starting a business peaks at least 1.5x the annual median in January, and a second, smaller peak sits in the four weeks before 31 January self-assessment, meaning more than half the year's revenue is available in about ten weeks.

**Why it matters.** It decides when to spend on acquisition and whether to build a subscription at all — a market that buys in two windows is badly served by a monthly subscription and well served by a dated, seasonal product.

**Test.** Google Trends, UK, five years, terms "start a business", "business ideas", "side hustle"; compute January index against the annual median for each year. Pass if January ≥1.5x median in at least four of five years. Cost: 1 hour, £0. Corroborate against the side-hustle motivation data, where cost of living is the top stated reason ([Finder UK](https://www.finder.com/uk/business-banking/side-hustle-statistics)).

**Prior. 0.75** — January new-year intent is one of the most reliable seasonal patterns in consumer search; the second peak is the uncertain half.

**What would change my mind.** A flat January in two or more of the five years, which would mean the demand is driven by personal shocks like redundancy that arrive on no calendar.

## 6. The market is shifting down the lanes: fewer companies, more side hustles

**Hypothesis.** UK company incorporations fall by a further 5% or more in the twelve months to June 2027, while side-hustle participation holds above 40% of adults — incorporations were **211,112 in Q1 2025** and **209,798 in Q2 2025**, against **204,612 in Q1 2026** and **192,287 in Q2 2026**, an 8.3% year-on-year fall in the June quarter ([Companies House Q1 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/incorporated-companies-in-the-uk-january-to-march-2026/incorporated-companies-in-the-uk-january-to-march-2026), [Q2 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/incorporated-companies-in-the-uk-april-to-june-2026/incorporated-companies-in-the-uk-april-to-june-2026)), while a Finder survey puts side hustles at **46% of UK adults in 2026, up from 39% in 2025** ([Finder UK](https://www.finder.com/uk/business-banking/side-hustle-statistics) — single-publisher consumer survey, treat as directional).

**Why it matters.** If the incorporating population is shrinking and the unincorporated one is growing, the lane mix and the price ladder are pointed at the wrong end of the market, and the `side_hustle` and `smb` lanes should carry the volume.

**Test.** Pull the last twelve Companies House quarterly releases and the ONS quarterly business demography series into one sheet, fit the trend, and check whether the fall survives the 2025/26 rule changes (identity verification and the fee rise are confounders that must be named). Pass if the fall is ≥5% and persists after excluding the quarter the fee changed. Cost: 4 hours, £0. Cross-check against ONS: **317,000 business births in 2024 against 280,000 deaths, the lowest death rate since 2016** ([ONS Business demography 2024](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/activitysizeandlocation/bulletins/businessdemography/2024)).

**Prior. 0.6** — two consecutive down quarters is a trend but not a proof, and Companies House anti-fraud reforms plausibly explain part of it.

**What would change my mind.** Q3 2026 incorporations back above 205,000, which would make the drop a rule-change artefact rather than a market shift.

## 7. What this market would actually buy is a survival probability, not an idea

**Hypothesis.** A survival estimate conditioned on industry code and region — buildable today from open ONS data, where **38.4% of businesses born in 2019 reached five years**, the South West reaching 43.5% and transport and storage showing both the highest birth rate at 15.6% and the highest death rate at 16.5% ([ONS Business demography 2024](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/activitysizeandlocation/bulletins/businessdemography/2024), [ONS five-year survival indicator](https://www.ons.gov.uk/explore-local-statistics/indicators/business-survival-five-year)) — commands at least twice the price of an idea dossier at the same effort.

**Why it matters.** It changes the unit sold from a document to a rating, which is renewable, comparable across ideas, and legible to a third party such as a lender or an insurer — the actuarial framing rather than the publishing one.

**Test.** Price test on live traffic: same idea, two checkout pages, one selling the dossier, one selling "your idea's five-year survival odds, with the ONS base rate and what moves it". 200 visitors each. Pass if the rating page's revenue per visitor is ≥2x. Cost: about £250 traffic, 8 hours build. Watch one trap: a base rate dressed as a personal forecast is an earnings-adjacent claim (see hypothesis 9).

**Prior. 0.4** — the framing is stronger, but a number that is honestly mostly a base rate may read as evasive once the buyer sees it.

**What would change my mind.** Buyers converting and then asking for a refund on discovering the estimate is a base rate, which would show the appeal was the illusion of specificity.

## 8. There is an institutional buyer already spending on exactly this decision

**Hypothesis.** UK institutions spend more than £20m a year assessing pre-launch business viability — the Start Up Loans programme has made **more than 125,000 loans worth over £1.2bn since 2012** ([British Business Bank](https://www.british-business-bank.co.uk/finance-hub/start-up-loan/), [Start Up Loans Scheme, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Start_Up_Loans_Scheme)), is scaling to **69,000 loans across the spending review period with average size rising from £12,000 to £15,000** ([British Business Bank, 31 July 2025](https://www.british-business-bank.co.uk/news-and-events/news/british-business-bank-is-allocated-more-than-p4.5bn-as-part-of-the-governments-backing-your-business-small-business-plan)), and every applicant must submit a business plan and cash-flow forecast that a delivery partner assesses ([Start Up Loans eligibility](https://www.startuploans.co.uk/support-and-guidance/frequently-asked-questions/eligibility)).

**Why it matters.** A delivery partner assessing tens of thousands of plans a year is a single contract that replaces thousands of consumer transactions, and it buys the kill log rather than the catalogue.

**Test.** Find the current delivery-partner list on the Start Up Loans site, contact three, and ask two questions: how many plans do you assess per adviser per week, and what does an assessment cost you. Pass if ≥2 answer and the implied cost per assessment is ≥£40. Cost: 8 hours over two weeks, £0.

**Prior. 0.35** — the spend clearly exists; whether a public-money programme can buy an outside machine verdict inside its own process is the doubtful half, and procurement would be slow.

**What would change my mind.** A delivery partner saying the assessment is mandated to be human, which would close the route regardless of price.

## 9. The market's legal shape is set abroad, and the exposure is the earnings claim

**Hypothesis.** The UK has no equivalent of the US Business Opportunity Rule, so no mandatory pre-sale disclosure or cooling-off period applies to selling business opportunities here — while in the US, any seller making an express or implied earnings claim must attach a substantiated Earnings Claim Statement and deliver the disclosure **seven calendar days** before signing ([16 CFR Part 437, eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-437), [FTC business guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/selling-work-home-or-other-business-opportunity-revised-rule-may-apply-you-1)).

**Why it matters.** Two opposite decisions hang off it: an open standard is available to define and own in the UK, and any income figure printed next to a purchasable idea is the single largest regulatory exposure the market carries — the FCA's posture towards unsubstantiated money claims online is not hypothetical, with a June 2025 week of action across nine regulators producing **3 arrests and 650 social media takedown requests** ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-leads-international-crackdown-illegal-finfluencers), [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-investigations/the-reel-cost-fca-cracks-down-on-finfluencers)).

**Test.** Two hours of desk research on the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations and the CAP Code's rules on money-making claims, then one hour of a solicitor's time to confirm no UK biz-opp disclosure regime exists. Pass if confirmed. Cost: 3 hours plus roughly £250. Then audit every income or revenue figure currently shown beside a purchasable item and check each has a cited substantiation — that audit is the deliverable, and it is cheap.

**Prior. 0.5** on the commercial half (that a disclosure standard is ownable); **0.85** that no UK equivalent rule exists.

**What would change my mind.** A UK regulator publishing guidance that treats vetted-opportunity sales as a regulated activity, which converts the open position into a licensing problem overnight.

## 10. Nobody in this market buys information; they buy a transfer of the downside — and that route is probably closed

**Hypothesis.** A guarantee that refunds the buyer's set-up costs if the idea fails within twelve months raises paid conversion by at least 3 percentage points, but constitutes a contract of insurance requiring FCA authorisation, so the commercially strongest offer in this market is the one that cannot legally be made without a permission.

**Why it matters.** It tells you whether the ceiling on this market is demand or regulation — and if it is regulation, the honest substitute (a partner who is already authorised) is a partnership decision, not a product one.

**Test.** Read FCA PERG 6 on the identification of contracts of insurance against the drafted wording, then one hour of regulatory counsel. Pass, meaning the route is closed, if counsel identifies it as insurance business. Cost: 3 hours plus roughly £300, and it can be done before any build. Insolvency data sets the loss frequency the guarantee would be priced against: **one in 199 UK companies entered insolvency in the twelve months to July 2026, 50.3 per 10,000** ([Insolvency Service, July 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/company-insolvencies-july-2026/commentary-company-insolvency-statistics-july-2026)), and that is insolvency alone, far below the roughly 62% of businesses that do not reach five years.

**Prior. 0.25** that a workable, unauthorised version exists — a performance warranty tied to our own service rather than to the buyer's business outcome is the narrow gap, and it is narrow.

**What would change my mind.** Counsel identifying a service-guarantee construction, conditioned on our own delivery rather than the buyer's trading result, that falls outside PERG 6.

**The one thing I did not expect:** every solvent payer in this market is standing on the *sell* side of it — lenders assessing plans, franchisors buying leads, platforms now handing HMRC £55bn of seller income — while the people the product is written for, the ones browsing for an idea, are by definition the segment with the least money and the least commitment, which is the same asymmetry that pushed credit ratings from subscriber-pays to issuer-pays fifty years ago.
